Deep learning · ocean physics

Predicting the ocean
you cannot see.

Aeolus infers subsurface seawater temperature down to 1,000 m, trained only on what satellites can observe at the surface — sea height, salinity, currents, and scatterometer winds.

Explore the repository See how it works

Named for the Greek keeper of the winds, Aeolus reads the surface signature the atmosphere leaves on the ocean and reconstructs the temperature structure hidden beneath it — a Residual Attention U-Net trained on reanalysis and scatterometer data, evaluated depth by depth.

Inputs

Two instruments, one ocean

Ocean physics reanalysis

0.083° resolution

  • thetaoSea water potential temperature
  • soSea water salinity
  • uo / voEastward & northward surface currents
  • zosSea surface height
  • mlotstMixed layer thickness
  • bottomTSea floor potential temperature

Scatterometer wind observations

0.25° / 20 km resolution

  • eastward_wind10 m stress-equivalent wind, U component
  • northward_wind10 m stress-equivalent wind, V component

Wind stress at the surface drives mixing and upwelling — the model learns to read that signal as a proxy for what's happening below.

Predicted across 15 standard depth levels (m)

Pipeline

From surface signal to depth profile

  1. 1

    Align & preprocess

    Resample the two grids onto a common frame, add normalized latitude/longitude channels (CoordConv) so the network stays location-aware, and mask out land before anything touches the loss.

  2. 2

    Residual Attention U-Net

    Channel and spatial attention (CBAM) inside every convolutional block sharpen the geospatial patterns that matter. A depth-residual head predicts each deeper layer as an offset from sea surface temperature, rather than from scratch.

  3. 3

    Depth-weighted training

    A masked loss reweights shallow versus deep water, where variance behaves very differently, alongside gradient clipping and best-checkpoint saving.

  4. 4

    Evaluate & export

    Per-depth RMSE, MAE and R² scores, rendered as horizontal slices, vertical temperature-depth profiles, and 3D ocean cuboids — then written out as NetCDF.

Output

What comes out the other end

Skill metrics

RMSE, MAE and R² computed independently at each of the 15 depth levels, so accuracy near the surface is never mistaken for accuracy at 1,000 m.

Visualizations

2D horizontal slices at fixed depth, vertical temperature-vs-depth profiles, and full 3D ocean cuboid renders for spatial context.

NetCDF export

Formatted, depth-indexed predictions saved in the format oceanographers already work with — ready to drop into existing tooling.
